### Paper Summary *

This paper analyzes a **population-level** formalization of Soft Actor-Critic (SAC): a **fixed-covariance squashed Gaussian** actor (linear mean, tanh push-forward) with **one projected reverse-KL gradient step** per outer iteration, and a **critic oracle** \(\hat Q_n\) with uniform error \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{crit}}\). The **target policy** is either the classical **Gibbs** (softmax of \(-\hat Q_n/\tau\), Eq. (4)) or a **policy mirror descent** target (Eq. (5)) with proximal step \(\lambda\), where the MD chain \(\{\pi_n\}\) is updated from the **previous target** (not from the current actor \(\pi_{w_n}\)). Under optimal-occupancy concentrability and a **Legendre-operator curvature** condition \(R_{\hat Q}/2 < \tau\), the authors prove strong convexity/smoothness of the actor KL objective, bound **target drift** and **actor tracking error**, and obtain a **best-iterate** regularized value gap \(O(N^{-1/5})\) (plus approximation error) when \(\lambda = \Theta(N^{-4/5})\). **Corollary 1** contrasts Gibbs vs MD: the Gibbs tracking bound retains a **non-vanishing** \(1/\tau\) term, whereas \(\lambda\) controls MD target drift. Appendix experiments on a solvable MDP illustrate failure when the curvature condition is violated.

### Critical Strengths

1. **Closes a gap between SAC practice and theory.** The analysis matches squashed Gaussian actors and partial KL updates (not abstract policy-space mirror descent) and yields an explicit **\(O(N^{-1/5})\)** best-iterate rate with a clear \(\lambda\)–\(N\) trade-off (Theorem 3).

2. **Quantitative Gibbs vs mirror-descent tracking.** Showing a **persistent \(1/\tau\)** contribution in the Gibbs tracking bound versus **\(\lambda\)-controlled drift** for MD targets is a derived, design-relevant distinction (Corollary 1, Lemma 2, Figure 2), not merely definitional.

### Critical Weaknesses

1. **Limited practical impact.** Population critic oracle, single exact projected actor step, and an unverifiable global curvature condition \(R_{\hat Q}/2 < \tau\) (only toy Fourier examples) mean the main rate is **conditional** for standard deep SAC; main-text experiments do not validate the **\(N^{-1/5}\)** rate on realistic training.

2. **MD tracking advantages are partly built into the target.** The \(O(\lambda)\) target drift follows from the \(1/\lambda\) KL penalty in Eq. (5); the incremental insight over “proximal targets move slowly” should be stated more explicitly, and the \(\pi_n\) target chain may not match all SAC implementations (target networks / actor-defined targets).

### Questions For Authors *

1. Can you provide **checkable sufficient conditions** (architecture, regularization, or online estimates) under which \(R_{\hat Q}/2 < \tau\) holds with high probability during learning?

2. How do the bounds change with **multiple actor gradient steps** per critic update (standard SAC), and is there a path to **joint stochastic actor–critic** analysis?

3. Please clarify how much of the mirror-vs-Gibbs tracking gap in Figure 2 is **intrinsic to the MD target definition** versus problem-independent constants in the tracking lemmas.

### Paper Summary *

The paper proposes **Q Normalizing Flows (QNF)**: the **entropy-regularized policy** is parameterized directly as a **normalizing flow** density, so the policy **is** the softmax of an implicit \(Q\) (Eqs. 11–12), rather than learning scalar \(Q(s,a)\) plus a separate actor regression (SAC/MPO). Training uses **soft TD** with twin critics and target networks; the flow is updated with **semi-gradient TD** (Eq. 15), distinct from Trust-PCL’s full gradient-TD. **\(\alpha\)** is tuned via closed-form **OLS** on \((\log\pi_\theta, \text{advantage})\) (Eq. 17). A **greedification** term (Eqs. 18–20, scale \(\lambda\)) pushes toward colder softmaps when TD residual vanishes. **QMANSF** (masked autoregressive neural spline flows) is evaluated on **8 DeepMind Control** tasks (500k steps, 5 seeds, IQM + bootstrap CIs) against **SAC, CrossQ, FMER, FPO, MEow**, with flow-architecture and \(\alpha\)/\(\lambda\) ablations. QNF is **competitive** on several tasks but **underperforms** SAC/CrossQ on exploitation-heavy tasks (e.g., cheetah-run, walker-run) and weak-exploration settings (e.g., hopper-stand). Scope is simulation-only, dense-reward focused.

### Critical Strengths

1. **Clean conceptual unification.** Representing the **softmax policy as the generative model** removes SAC-style separate policy-improvement regression while keeping standard off-policy TD machinery.

2. **Training mechanisms with ablations.** **\(\alpha\) OLS autotuning** and **greedification** address fixed-\(\alpha\) sensitivity (Figure 4) and stagnation at \(\lambda=0\), with experiments matching the narrative.
