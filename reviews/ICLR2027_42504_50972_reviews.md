# Submission 42504 — *An Analysis of Mirror-Descent Soft Actor-Critic*

## Extended review (English)

### 1. What problem is this paper trying to solve?

Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) is the de facto off-policy algorithm for continuous control with entropy regularization. In code, each outer loop typically:

- updates critics with soft Bellman / TD targets;
- defines a **target policy** from the current Q-estimate (softmax / Gibbs form);
- performs **one or a few** gradient steps on the actor to minimize reverse KL to that target—not a full convex projection.

Theory, by contrast, often assumes either (i) updates in **full policy space** with known critic (policy mirror descent, exponential convergence lines of work), or (ii) **direct policy gradient** with regularity that squashed Gaussians violate near action boundaries. This paper asks: *If we formalize SAC at the population level with a squashed Gaussian actor and a critic oracle, what finite-time guarantees can we prove when the target is either the classical Gibbs policy or a policy-mirror-descent (MD) target—and how do those targets differ for **tracking**?*

The contribution is **not** a new deep RL algorithm. It is a **mathematical model** of SAC-like training plus **comparative bounds** on target drift and actor tracking, culminating in a **best-iterate** \(O(N^{-1/5})\) rate (up to approximation error) when \(\lambda\) is tuned with \(N\).

### 2. Setting and notation (compressed but precise)

- **MDP:** infinite horizon, cost \(c\), discount \(\gamma\), actions \(A = (-1,1)^M\), entropy temperature \(\tau > 0\).
- **Regularized value** \(V_\tau^\pi(s)\): expected discounted cost minus \(\tau\) times per-state entropy of \(\pi(\cdot|s)\).
- **Actor:** fixed diagonal covariance Gaussian in pre-tanh space, **linear mean** \(m_w(s) = x(s)^\top w\) (feature map \(x\)), then tanh push-forward to bounded actions. This matches the “Gaussian + squash” SAC parameterization at population level.
- **Critic oracle:** at iteration \(n\), the learner receives \(\hat Q_n\) with uniform error \(\|\hat Q_n - Q_\tau^{\pi_{w_n}}\|_\infty \le \varepsilon_{\mathrm{crit}}\) (population, not mini-batch TD).
- **Replay / occupancy:** state distribution \(\rho_n\) evolves (Definition 5 in paper); **concentrability** (Assumption 1) bounds mismatch between successive occupancies—standard in batch/off-policy theory.
- **Actor step:** one projected gradient step with step size \(h\) on reverse KL objective \(J_n(w, \pi_n^{\mathrm{target}})\) toward the current target, with projection to ball \(B(R)\).

### 3. The two targets (this is the conceptual core)

**Gibbs target (Eq. 4, “classical SAC soft policy”):**  
\(\pi_{n+1}^G(\cdot|s) \propto \exp(-\hat Q_n(s,\cdot)/\tau)\) (with appropriate base measure on \(A\)). This is the closed-form solution of the **one-step** entropy-regularized improvement problem when the critic is exact. In continuous actions the normalization constant is intractable in general, but the **gradient** of the actor objective still avoids needing \(Z_n(s)\) when defined carefully (paper’s SAC motivation).

**Mirror-descent target (Eq. 5):**  
A **proximal** update: \(\pi_{n+1}\) is the minimizer of a KL-regularized linear functional of \(\hat Q_n\) **anchored at the previous target** \(\pi_n\) (not at the current actor \(\pi_{w_n}\)). Step size / penalty \(\lambda\) controls how far the target moves per outer iteration. Intuitively:

- Gibbs: each target is “fresh softmax of \(-\hat Q/\tau\)” — can jump when \(\hat Q\) or \(\rho_n\) changes.
- MD: target chain \(\{\pi_n\}\) is **inertial**; large \(\lambda\) → aggressive improvement; small \(\lambda\) → slowly moving target.

**Why targets “change” at all:** the critic \(\hat Q_n\) changes as \(w_n\) and \(\rho_n\) change; the paper separates **target drift** (how fast \(\pi_n^{\mathrm{target}}\) moves) from **actor tracking error** (how well one KL gradient step follows that target).

### 4. Curvature condition (Theorem 2) — what it means

The reverse-KL actor objective is generally **non-convex** in \(w\) because of tanh and because \(\hat Q\) enters through the target log-density. The paper introduces a **Legendre-type operator** \(T\) applied to \(\hat Q(s,\cdot)\) in action space and a scalar **curvature** \(R_{\hat Q}\) (sup of operator norms across states).

**Key condition:** \(R_{\hat Q}/2 < \tau\).

When this holds on a ball of radius \(R\), the actor objective \(w \mapsto J_n(w, \pi_{n+1})\) is **strongly convex and smooth** with explicit constants \(\kappa\) and \(L\). Then a **single** gradient step analysis is tractable (like convex tracking).

**Interpretation:** entropy \(\tau\) must dominate “how bent” the critic looks along the squashed action map. Violating this matches Appendix/Figure 1 **orange** curves: training can fail or stall even with equal \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{crit}}\).

**Gap for practitioners:** only **Fourier-linear critic** examples give checkable bounds on \(R_{\hat Q}\); for deep nets there is no recipe to verify or monitor this during training.

### 5. Main results (prose)

| Result | Role |
|--------|------|
| **Theorem 1 (MD tracking)** | Bounds time-averaged actor suboptimality for MD target; drift enters through \(O(\lambda)\) target movement (Lemma 9) and \(1/\lambda\) terms from MD progress (trade-off). |
| **Corollary 1 (Gibbs tracking)** | Same tracking functional for Gibbs target; bound includes **non-vanishing \(1/\tau\)** piece even when replay changes slowly—actor cannot track arbitrarily closely purely by slowing data shift. |
| **Theorem 3 (best-iterate value gap)** | With \(\lambda = \Theta(N^{-4/5})\), \(h\) small enough, best iterate over \(N\) actor updates achieves \(V_\tau^{\pi_{w_r}}(\rho) - V_\tau^{\pi^*}(\rho) \le O(N^{-1/5} + \varepsilon_{\mathrm{approx}}(N))\). |

**Rate \(N^{-1/5}\):** slower than “nice” convex \(O(1/N)\) or exponential MD in tabular exact-critic settings; reflects **partial** actor steps + evolving target + approximation errors bundled into \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{approx}}\).

**Figure 2 (Appendix K):** with **exact** critics on a toy MDP, average tracking error vs \(\lambda\): MD target (solid) vs Gibbs (dashed) at matched actor budgets \(n\). Gibbs error plateaus; MD error can shrink as \(\lambda \downarrow\) (at cost of slower MD policy improvement via \(1/\lambda\) in Theorem 1).

### 6. Related work (how I place this paper)

- **Policy MD / entropy RL (Lan; Ju & Lan; Kerimkulov et al.):** exact or high-accuracy critic, updates in policy space—fast rates but not squashed Gaussian + partial steps.
- **Parametric SAC analysis (Sherman, Alfano, Yuan, Agarwal lines):** closer to implementation but different objective/target choices; this paper explicitly compares **Gibbs vs MD target** under one actor formalism.
- **Actor–critic with critic error (Labbi, Zorba et al.):** complementary; this work **fixes** critic error to \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{crit}}\) and studies actor/target coupling.

**Novelty assessment:** incremental but real for **SAC theory**—first population guarantees for squashed Gaussian + one reverse-KL step + explicit **target-type comparison**. Less novel as a general RL algorithm paper.

### 7. Experiments (what they actually show)

Appendix K only: tabular state, quadratic cost, affine transitions, **exact linear critic solve** + controlled perturbations to vary \(R_{\hat Q}\) at fixed \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{crit}}\). Figure 1: value vs iterations when curvature condition satisfied (green) vs violated (orange). **No MuJoCo/DMC**, no neural nets, no validation of \(N^{-1/5}\) scaling in log-log plots.

### 8. Extended strengths

1. **Implementation-aligned actor:** fixed-covariance squashed Gaussian + single projected reverse-KL step is exactly the fragile part of SAC omitted from policy-space MD proofs.
2. **Explicit Gibbs vs MD tracking dichotomy (Corollary 1):** the persistent \(1/\tau\) term is a derived contrast, not folklore; Figure 2 supports the mechanism on toy data.
3. **Curvature narrative links failure modes to \(\hat Q\) geometry** via Legendre operator—conceptually useful even if hard to test in deep RL.
4. **Clear \(\lambda\)–\(N\) trade-off** in Theorem 3 (\(\lambda \sim N^{-4/5}\)) for best-iterate value bound—rare explicit tuning exponent in SAC-like analyses.
5. **Writing quality:** assumptions numbered, algorithm box, proof roadmap in appendix—easy for theory readers to navigate 36 pages.

### 9. Extended weaknesses

1. **Population critic oracle** hides TD bootstrapping, target networks, double Q, and non-stationary replay—main theorems are conditional on \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{crit}}\), not on sample complexity.
2. **Single actor gradient step** vs standard SAC (often multiple actor updates per env step); no theorem for “\(K\) inner steps.”
3. **\(R_{\hat Q}/2 < \tau\)** is strong and **unverifiable** for deep critics; experiments only show binary satisfy/violate on toy Fourier critics.
4. **MD target chain \(\pi_n\)** may not match every SAC codebase (some implementations implicitly define target from current actor or use separate slow-moving target networks on Q, not on policy MD chain).
5. **Part of MD advantage is definitional:** small \(\lambda\) ⇒ small KL proximal step ⇒ \(O(\lambda)\) drift—paper acknowledges via \(1/\lambda\) in Theorem 1 but could foreground how much is **design of Eq. (5)** vs inherent to entropy RL.
6. **Limited ICLR audience payoff:** no actionable hyperparameter rule beyond qualitative “small \(\lambda\) helps tracking”; \(N^{-1/5}\) is not sharp enough to guide wall-clock training.

### 10. Recommendation rationale

- **For RL theory subcommunity:** weak accept — fills a documented gap between SAC practice and MD theory.
- **For general ICLR (empirical + systems):** borderline — no deep RL evidence, limited engineering takeaway.

## Extended review — 42504 (中文)

### 1. 问题与动机

SAC 在连续控制中广泛使用，但理论常假设 policy 空间更新或光滑 policy gradient，而实现上是 **tanh 压扁高斯 + 每轮仅少量 reverse-KL 步**。本文在 **population** 层面对齐这一实现，并比较两类 **target**：经典 **Gibbs**（\(\exp(-\hat Q/\tau)\)）与 **policy mirror descent**（式 (5)，步长 \(\lambda\)），研究 **target 漂移** 与 **actor tracking** 如何影响有限步收敛。

### 2. 两类 target 为何不同

- **Gibbs**：每步从当前 \(\hat Q_n\) 重新做 softmax，\(\hat Q\) 或 occupancy 一变，target 可跳变；tracking 界中出现 **不随迭代消失的 \(1/\tau\)**（推论 1）。
- **MD**：target 链 \(\pi_{n+1}\) 由 **上一 target** 经 KL  proximal 更新得到；**\(\lambda\) 小**则 target 动得慢，actor 更易跟踪，但 MD 本身 policy improvement 变慢（定理 1 中 **\(1/\lambda\)** 项）。

### 3. 曲率条件

**\(R_{\hat Q}/2 < \tau\)** 时 reverse-KL actor 子问题在 \(B(R)\) 上强凸/光滑，才能分析 **单步** 梯度跟踪。深度 critic 难以验证；附录仅 Fourier 线性 critic 有例。图 1 在固定 \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{crit}}\) 下比较满足/违反曲率。

### 4. 主结论与实验

定理 3：**best-iterate** 值差距 **\(O(N^{-1/5})\)**（加逼近误差），取 **\(\lambda=\Theta(N^{-4/5})\)**。实验仅在可精确解 critic 的玩具 MDP（附录 K），**无** 深度 RL。

### 5. 贡献强弱判断

对 **SAC 理论** 有填补；对 **广义 ICLR** 工程启示有限。MD 相对 Gibbs 的部分优势来自 **target 定义** 与 \(\lambda\) 权衡，作者应更明确增量 novelty。

# Submission 50972 — *Q-Learning with Normalizing Flows* (QNF)

## Extended review (English)

### 1. Motivation

Discrete Q-learning uses **max** over actions; continuous control replaces max with **softmax** over actions weighted by \(\exp(Q(s,a)/\alpha)\), but then you need either (i) a separate actor to represent that distribution, or (ii) a generative model that **is** the softmax. SAC/CrossQ/MPO: learn \(Q\) (or critic) + **policy extraction** (regression to softmax). **QNF** parameterizes the **policy density directly** as a **normalizing flow**, so the policy **is** the implicit softmax of a scalar potential—avoiding an explicit “actor improvement” network that chases a moving softmax target.

### 2. Method pipeline

- **Policy:** flow \(f_\theta(s,a)\) with tractable density \(\pi_\theta(a|s)\); interpret as softmax of implicit Q (Eqs. 11–12 in paper).
- **Critics:** twin Q (or value + advantage decomposition as in algorithm figure); **target networks** for stability.
- **Update:** **semi-gradient soft TD** on flow parameters (Eq. 15)—distinct from Trust-PCL-style **full** gradient through the Bellman operator.
- **Entropy temperature \(\alpha\):** not fixed grid only—**closed-form OLS** each step on pairs \((\log \pi_\theta(a|s), \text{advantage})\) (Eq. 17), “stateless greedy” autotune.
- **Greedification (Eqs. 18–20):** extra term with scale \(\lambda\) pushes policy toward colder / more peaked distribution when TD residual small—addresses stagnation when \(\lambda=0\).

**QMANSF:** masked autoregressive **neural spline flow**—authors’ main architecture; alternatives QMAF, QRNVP, QCNSF, QEBFlow, QMLP, QGauss compared in Fig. 3.

### 3. Baselines and protocol

- **8 DeepMind Control** tasks, **500k** env steps, **5 seeds**, report **IQM** + **95% bootstrap CI** (Agarwal et al. 2021).
- Baselines: **SAC**, **CrossQ**, **FMER**, **FPO**, **MEow** (flow-related / entropy RL methods).
- **Shared hyperparameters across envs** for all methods (simplicity but may hurt fairness).
- **Stochastic evaluation** for all (flows lack deterministic mode)—fair to flows, may hurt exploitation-heavy comparison vs deterministic eval SAC.

### 4. Results (detailed read)

- **Figure 2 (main comparison):** QNF **competitive** on several locomotion/manipulation tasks but **lags SAC/CrossQ** on **exploitation-heavy** runners (**cheetah-run**, **walker-run**) and on **hopper-stand** (weak reward / need exploration)—authors acknowledge CrossQ adapts entropy setpoint while QNF with greedification may **wait for signal** and fail to discover stand-up behavior.
- **Figure 3:** **QMANSF** among best flow variants—few layers, high expressivity per layer.
- **Figure 4:** **\(\alpha\) sensitivity** high across envs; autotune OLS matches best fixed \(\alpha\) in many cases; **\(\lambda=0.1\)** reasonable for greed; large \(\lambda\) can **collapse** policy.

### 5. Related positioning

- **MEow / energy-based flows (NeurIPS 2024 line):** also flow + entropy RL; QNF differs by **direct Q-flow** + semi-gradient TD + OLS \(\alpha\) + greedification.
- **Trust-PCL / soft Q with flows:** gradient-type differences matter for bias/variance—paper argues semi-gradient is practical.

### 6. Extended strengths

1. **Conceptual clarity:** one generative model replaces actor–critic “two-headed” softmax extraction; Figure 1 algorithm is easy to teach.
2. **Training knobs with ablations:** \(\alpha\) OLS and greed \(\lambda\) are not afterthoughts—Fig. 4 supports narrative.
3. **Architecture study (Fig. 3)** within QNF family—QMANSF justified, not arbitrary.
4. **Honest limitation section:** sim-only, dense reward, compute vs Gaussian actor, stability tricks (split quadratic solve).
5. **Reproducibility claim:** anonymous code link promised—important if baselines are sensitive (SAC \(\alpha\) init 0.2 vs official 1.0 noted in text).

### 7. Extended weaknesses

1. **Abstract vs evidence:** “perform similarly to widely used actor-critic” is **overstated** where CrossQ/SAC win clearly on multiple reported tasks.
2. **No wall-clock / sample-normalized compute**—flows cost more per step; unclear if gaps shrink with equal wall time.
3. **Single hyperparameter sheet across 8 envs**—may under-tune SAC/CrossQ or over-tune QNF; MEow from Hopper defaults + manual entropy tweak—**baseline fairness** weak.
4. **No theory** for semi-gradient TD + flows; no convergence or bias characterization.
5. **Scope:** no sparse reward, high-D action, real robots; greedification may harm exploration—hopper-stand is a red flag for deployment.
6. **Statistical testing:** CIs shown but few formal tests vs strongest baseline per task.

### 8. Recommendation rationale

**Borderline accept:** interesting unification and reasonable engineering effort for 15 pages, but empirical headline oversells and key tasks show **clear baseline wins**. Would strengthen with matched-compute tables and per-task tuned baselines.
