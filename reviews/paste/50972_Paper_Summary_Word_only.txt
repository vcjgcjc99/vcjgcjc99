Continuous maximum-entropy Q-learning requires representing a softmax policy over actions, but standard actor-critic pipelines first learn a scalar Q(s,a) (or critics) and then run a separate policy extraction step to fit the softmax (SAC, CrossQ, MPO). This paper proposes Q Normalizing Flows (QNF): parameterize the policy density directly with a normalizing flow, so the policy is the normalized exponential of an implicit state-action potential (Eqs. 11–12), eliminating an explicit actor regression head while keeping off-policy soft temporal-difference learning with twin critics and target networks.

Training applies semi-gradient TD to flow parameters (Eq. 15), which the authors contrast with full gradient-TD through the Bellman backup as in Trust-PCL. Entropy temperature alpha is adapted each step via a closed-form OLS fit on (log pi_theta(a|s), advantage) pairs (Eq. 17), motivated by strong sensitivity to fixed alpha across environments (Figure 4). Greedification (Eqs. 18–20, weight lambda) adds a term that pushes the policy toward colder, more peaked distributions when TD error is small, addressing stagnation at lambda=0.

The primary architecture QMANSF is a masked autoregressive neural spline flow. Experiments on eight DeepMind Control domains use 500k environment steps, five random seeds, and report inter-quartile mean (IQM) returns with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals. Baselines include SAC, CrossQ, FMER, FPO, MEow, with flow-family comparisons (Figure 3) and alpha/lambda ablations (Figure 4). QNF is competitive on several tasks but lags SAC/CrossQ on exploitation-heavy runners (cheetah-run, walker-run) and on hopper-stand. The study is simulation-only and dense-reward focused; limitations note higher compute, flow instability mitigations, and lack of sparse/high-dimensional benchmarks.
